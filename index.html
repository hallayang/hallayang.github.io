About

I work in quantitative research, with long-standing interests in machine learning and artificial intelligence. From 2017-2025, I was at Citadel, where I ultimately led the Data Strategies Group and worked on data science and quantitative research. Prior to Citadel, I was a consultant at McKinsey, a quant at Jump Trading, and a vice president at Goldman Sachs. I received a Ph.D. in Business Economics from Harvard Business School and a B.A. from Harvard in Physics, summa cum laude.

My recent research spans neural architectures for computer vision, 3D structural biology, program synthesis for reasoning tasks, and applications of transformers to complex problems, with a particular focus on reasoning systems.

Research & projects

Vesuvius Challenge - Surface Detection

22 / 1391

In 79 AD, Mount Vesuvius buried the city of Herculaneum and carbonized a library of papyrus scrolls into what looks like a collection of charred logs. For nearly two thousand years, nobody could read them. The Vesuvius Challenge is trying to change that by using CT scanning and machine learning to virtually unwrap and read these scrolls without physically unrolling them, which would destroy them. I trained models that would generate topologically sound scroll segments, finishing 22nd out of 1391 participants in a Kaggle competition.

  • Convolutional Neural Networks
  • Surface Segmentation
  • Topological Features

ARC-AGI Challenge

research

Visual puzzles requiring reasoning and symbolic knowledge are straightforward for humans to solve, but a challenge for even frontier LLM models. I explored approaches to solving this problem based on both Bonnet and Macfarlane's Latent Program Networks, and also Jolicoeur-Martineau's Tiny Recursive Models. See this New York Times article from March 2025 for a description of the challenge.

  • Visual Reasoning
  • Program Synthesis
  • Transformers

RNA 3D Folding

24 / 1516

Google DeepMind was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 2024 for their work on protein structure prediction, but the problem of RNA 3D structure prediction remains open due to greater difficulty and fewer experimentally resolved structures usable for training data. Stanford's Rhiju Das hosted a Kaggle competition to stimulate research, and I explored methods based on both AlphaFold and language models to try to improve the state of the art. My approach relied neither on templates nor MSAs, and finished 24th of 1516 contestants in the first Stanford competition, and 48th of 1867 contestants in the second Stanford competition.

  • Transformers
  • Diffusion Models
  • Structural Biology

AI Mathematical Olympiad

34 / 2213

Can LLMs solve Mathematical Olympiad level problems? While OpenAI and other foundational model trainers have pushed the limits of what can be done given infinite resources, this competition posed the question of what could be implemented on 4xL4 GPUs in a finite amount of compute time. I finished 34th of 2213 on the Kaggle leaderboard for the "Progress Prize 2" competition (closed April 1, 2025).

  • Large Language Models
  • Problem Solving
  • Efficient Inference
